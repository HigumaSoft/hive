# Ready

"Ready" means the owner is ready to move forward in this project. The Round Dancer answers it, in this order:

1. **Checks the project.** Unless `docs/hive-profile.yaml` says `setup: done`, it follows `new-project.md` and does nothing else. If setup is done but a profile field is missing or empty, it fills just that field the way setup would, keeping every other value.
2. **Finds what is due.** A meeting on the org calendar is due when it was planned for today or earlier and its Confluence page doesn't say Done or Cancelled: what counts is whether it is finished, not its date. Some items come due without a calendar event: a sprint that has ended makes its review, retro and planning due (this project's items); a skill search is due monthly (`skills.md`), an org review quarterly (`meetings.md`) and the Scent Guard's check weekly or per sprint (`colony/scent-guard.md`) (org items). A recurring item first falls due one period after its rule was accepted, then one period after it was last held; "ready" works it out from the rule's date in `decisions.md` or from the last meeting page. An item with no time yet is offered like the others. No time is set by inference.
3. **Puts the due items in order:** priority first (Urgent, Regular, Low; a consultation counts as Low), then the date planned, then the type (org, then training, then project), then dependencies: an item that another depends on comes first, and an item whose dependency isn't done waits. The calendar time only breaks a remaining tie. A meeting whose participants include a Symbiont not yet in the pool isn't started: the Queen meeting that creates it is offered instead.
4. **Offers the first item and stops there,** with one line for each other item due. It doesn't go on to tickets, branches or waiting work while a meeting is due. It assumes the owner has time, so it offers the meeting to start, not to skip. If the owner skips it or gives it a new time anyway, the next item is offered and the items that depend on it are held back. A skipped item comes back at the next "ready"; a new time updates its page and calendar event. A training meeting can also be dropped; an org item can't.
5. **Hands the session to the bee the meeting page names under "Run by":** the Waggle Dancer for most org meetings, Queen for changes to hive, the Nurse for training, and for a focused project session the bee whose field it is. The Round Dancer reads that bee's file in `colony/` and works as it. This handover happens only here, at the start of the session, before any other work. Meetings of other projects are only mentioned: they run in their own repo and never block this one.
6. **When nothing is due,** it lists what is waiting in this project (paused work and open flags), then takes the next ticket.

The meeting the owner starts fills the session (`meetings.md`); after it, a new session and a new "ready" move on.
